# Full-intensity preset: prevent the polite version

Read this for the default “go all out” brief. A large hero plus a few readouts
can still be sparse. A first independent test of this skill produced exactly that:
coherent financial strata, some filaments, and too little else. Use this production
plan to avoid confusing a valid 3D exhibit with sensory overload.

## Declare the visual systems before coding

Make a spectacle manifest: each row names a visible system, its frame region,
geometry/material treatment, movement, and response at the climax. For this preset,
plan **one monumental hero and at least three substantial secondary systems**, plus
fine instrumental texture and optical atmosphere. A secondary system is visually
substantial, not a tiny status light, another line of text, or more identical dust.

Example financial manifestation:

| System | Physical form | Continuous motion | Climax |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liquidity architecture | Stratified cliffs with several assembly types | Local level reconfiguration | Layers unfold into separated fans |
| Volatility volume | Suspended curved wireframe/surface over the canyon | Traveling ridges and slices | Deforms into a funnel above the spread |
| Cross-venue circulation | Large tubular or ribbon trajectories spanning the frame | Flow packets and directional traces | Branches redirect into the exposed interior |
| Execution waterfall | Dense luminous ladder/wall with depth and irregular blocks | Rows sweep and cascade | A shockfront tears down its height |
| Price artery | Thick luminous trajectory with subordinate strands | Flow with varied speed and phase | Splits, rises, then rejoins downstream |
| Technical surface | Tick friezes, local cross-sections, terminal glyphs, tiny schemas | Scanners and propagating highlights | Several regions respond in sequence |
| Optics / depth | Reflective mass, local haze, near/far layers | Moving light and restrained camera | Interior light reveals a previously hidden volume |

This is an example, not a requirement to copy a canyon. For another subject choose
equally substantial systems. The crucial change is multiple visually different
mechanisms operating in one composition. Do not substitute seven varieties of particles.

## Manufacture variation at the middle scale

Repeated slabs, rings, or cubes do not supply enough craft alone. Give the hero
several assembly types: interrupted terraces, suspended sections, articulated fins,
exposed inner lattices, ribbed connectors, nested cross-sections, material inlays.
These are distinct structures, not random additions. Large masses establish shape;
middle-scale assemblies make it feel designed; tiny parts make it feel expensive.

Use instancing/merged geometry to afford the density. Keep a readable silhouette
and dark cavities, but do not let “negative space” become an excuse for half the
screen being unoccupied blackness in a sensory-overload brief. Sparse breathing
room belongs around focal contrast, not where a promised system was never built.

## A transformation must change the shot

Compare pre-event and peak frames at phone-thumbnail size. The composition or
silhouette must visibly change. A 0.1-radian tilt, a slight spacing increase, and
brighter lines are usually insufficient for the headline event.

Examples: strata unfold into a fan revealing a nested order lattice; a suspended
surface becomes a funnel and redirects flows; an opaque instrument separates into
exploded layers; an apparently solid monolith opens to reveal moving internal
architecture. Anticipate the event, keep it readable for a moment, and overlap the
release with continuing secondary motion. Never make it a full-screen flash.

## Make the intended materials actually visible

If the render is mostly edge lines on black, fix physical material response before
adding labels. Metallic surfaces need an environment and readable light directions.
The [optics helper](../assets/optics.mjs) supplies a PMREM environment, key/rim light,
and a correctly ordered bloom/output pipeline. Copy it into the target project;
adapt its colors and exposure. It is a reusable starting point, not a required style.

At normal display brightness, the viewer should see surface planes and assemblies,
not have to infer them from hairline outlines. Keep dark mass, but avoid crushing
all structural information. Over-bright emissive detail can be just as destructive.

## Do not prematurely convert failure into delivery

Render the hero-only composition before spending the bulk of the effort on HUD
copy, selectors, README prose, or tests. Then build the complete performance and
inspect it again. If the result is coherent but restrained, add the missing
substantial systems or rebuild the transformation; don't declare that the lower bar
was the intent. With no user-imposed time limit, a failed visual gate means another
targeted build/render pass, not an invented deadline. If genuinely blocked or
explicitly budget-limited, report the artistic result as incomplete.
